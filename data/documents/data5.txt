Search Engines, Ranking, and Relevance

A search engine has to deal with a large collection of documents and a large number of user queries. Indexing makes retrieval efficient by allowing the system to locate documents containing a query term without scanning every document.

Retrieval and ranking are related but different tasks. Retrieval determines which documents satisfy the query, while ranking determines the order in which retrieved documents should be presented to the user.

Term frequency measures how often a term occurs in a document. Inverse document frequency gives more importance to terms that occur in fewer documents in the collection. The combination of term frequency and inverse document frequency is commonly called tf-idf.

A term that appears in almost every document may be less useful for distinguishing relevant documents. A term that appears in only a small number of documents can provide more information about which documents are relevant.

The vector space model represents documents and queries using term weights. Similarity measures can then be used to compare a query with documents. These scoring methods can improve search results when a simple Boolean match is not enough.

Even when ranking is used, the inverted index remains important because it provides the candidate documents and term information needed during query processing.
